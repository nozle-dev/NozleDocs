---
title: Dunning policy
description: Grace, retries, reminders and the block of a customer whose invoices stay unpaid
---

A dunning policy decides what happens to one of your customers while an invoice stays unpaid: when Nozle retries the charge, when it reminds the customer, and what it does when the grace ends. This page is about your customers. For what your own workspace pays Nozle, see [Workspace billing](/guides/getting-started/workspace-billing).

Dunning works for every workspace, on every [Nozle plan](/guides/getting-started/nozle-plans#premium-features). From Core 11.42.0 it is no longer a paid add-on, and a new workspace starts with a [default policy](#the-default-policy), so a customer who does not pay is blocked once the grace ends without any setup from you. Open **Billing > Dunning** in the dashboard to change it or create other campaigns. A customer excluded from dunning is never dunned.

## The default policy

_Core 11.42.0._ Every new workspace, and every new sandbox, gets one dunning campaign when it is created:

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Code and name | `default`, "Default dunning policy" |
| End action | `block` |
| Threshold | 0, in the currency of your default billing entity |
| Applied to | Your default billing entity |
| Customer email | `dunning.step` is switched on for the default billing entity |

So an unpaid invoice of any customer of the default billing entity follows the [timeline](#the-timeline) below, and the customer is blocked when the grace of the plan ends (7 days unless the plan sets another). Edit the campaign like any other, or apply another campaign to a plan, a customer or a billing entity.

- A sandbox blocks as live does, but never emails a customer.
- Workspaces created before Core 11.42.0 do not get the default policy automatically. Turn on a policy yourself as described below.

## Turn the policy on

A policy is a dunning campaign with an **end action**. Today the only end action is `block`: once the grace of an unpaid invoice ends, the customer's usage is refused until the invoice is paid.

- A campaign without an end action keeps the attempt cadence it has today (days between attempts, maximum attempts). Nothing changes for it.
- Set the end action in the dashboard on the campaign's **End action** (**Classic cadence** or **Block after the plan's grace**), or with the GraphQL field `endAction` (`DunningCampaignEndActionEnum`, value `block`) on `createDunningCampaign` or `updateDunningCampaign`. `null` (the default, **Classic cadence**) means no policy.
- The grace comes from the plan: `unpaid_invoice_grace_days`, 0 to 90 days, 7 by default. See [Grace days on a plan](/guides/billing/plans#grace-days-and-dunning).
- A policy honours the campaign's thresholds, per currency (Core 11.42.0). An invoice is dunned only when what the customer owes in its currency reaches one of the campaign's thresholds in that currency. Below it no step is sent and no block is set, and a block already set is lifted. A currency for which the campaign has no threshold is still dunned. The default policy's threshold of 0 duns every unpaid invoice.

### Which campaign an invoice follows

Each unpaid invoice follows the first campaign found in this order:

1. The campaign of the plan behind the invoice (`dunning_campaign_code` on the plan).
2. The campaign applied to the customer.
3. The campaign applied to the customer's billing entity.
4. The campaign of your default billing entity.

The policy applies when that campaign has an end action. A customer with several subscriptions has one timeline per unpaid invoice, each with the grace of the plan behind that invoice. An invoice without a plan uses 7 days. The invoice of a plan change bills the replaced plan and the new one: it follows the campaign and grace of the new plan.

An unpaid prepaid credit top-up is never dunned (Core 11.42.0). It granted no credits, so it does not block the customer.

An invoice whose amount due is below the payment provider's minimum charge is never dunned: its amount is carried to the customer's next invoice. See [Amounts below the provider minimum](/guides/billing/invoices#amounts-below-the-provider-minimum).

## The timeline

The first charge is tried as soon as the invoice is finalized, within seconds, not after a wait. Day 0 of an invoice is the day that charge failed, or the moment it is past its due date and has stayed unpaid for one hour without a charge waiting for the bank. A debit that is still processing (for example an Indian card during the bank's notice) is not a failure and does not start day 0.

With the default grace of 7 days:

| Day | Step |
|---|---|
| 0 | `payment_failed`: webhook, and the customer email when it is on (it replaces the failed payment email, see [Customer emails](#customer-emails)). |
| 1 | Retry the charge, only after a soft decline. |
| 3 | `reminder`. |
| 5 | Retry the charge, only after a soft decline. |
| 6 | `final_reminder`, with the deadline. |
| 7 | `end_action`: the customer is blocked. |
| Any day | `resolved` once the invoice no longer needs paying. The block lifts at once. |

For another grace, the reminder falls on `round(grace × 3 / 7)`, the final reminder on the day before the end, and the end action on the last day. Retries stay on days 1 and 5 when they fall before the end. With a grace of 0, the charge is still tried first: a customer whose charge succeeds is never blocked, and one whose charge fails is blocked on day 0 with no retries (the block lands at the next hourly run).

Nozle never retries a charge that cannot succeed without the customer: an authentication request, no usable mandate, an amount above the mandate's ceiling, or no payment method at all. It never retries a hard decline either (for example a lost, stolen or expired card). Failures are classified from the payment provider's structured decline codes, never from message text. When the customer saves a new valid default payment method, every invoice of that customer the policy runs on is retried at once, and the block lifts as soon as they are paid. An Indian card whose mandate becomes inactive does not trigger this retry.

## What resolves it

The block clears as soon as no invoice past its grace is owed and not being debited:

- the invoice is paid (pay link, a retry, a new payment method),
- it is voided or covered by a credit note,
- or an automatic debit of it is in flight (the block comes back if that debit fails).

The `resolved` step is sent once the invoice no longer needs paying.

## Lost disputes

_Core 11.42.0 and 11.45.0._ When a customer wins a card dispute (a chargeback) on a paid invoice, the bank takes the money back. Nozle treats what the dispute took as owed again, so your dunning policy can collect it and block the customer again until it is settled.

- **Only the disputed amount is owed.** A 100.00 dispute on a 300.00 payment makes 100.00 owed, not 300.00. A refund made before the dispute and a payment the dispute did not take back are not owed again.
- **The policy runs at once.** Losing the dispute runs the customer's dunning policy straight away, not at the next hourly run. A blocked customer stays blocked, and gets no "All good" email, until the owed amount is settled.
- **`total_due_amount_cents` is what is owed again.** It is what the disputes took, plus anything still due before, less payments recorded since and offset credit notes, and never below 0. The REST API, GraphQL, the invoice webhooks, [`dunning.step`](#the-dunningstep-webhook), the dunning emails and the plan change check all use this one number. Before Core 11.45.0 it was 0 on such an invoice.
- **`disputed_amount_cents`** is new on the invoice (0 without a lost dispute) and on the payment (`null` when no dispute was lost). It is what lost disputes took back.
- **`total_paid_amount_cents` stays gross.** After a recovery it can be higher than `total_amount_cents`. A 300.00 invoice that was paid, fully disputed and paid again reads 600.00.

Ways to settle it:

- The customer pays what is owed online. _Core 11.46.0._ A payment URL (`POST /invoices/{id}/payment_url`) or an invoice checkout through Stripe or Razorpay works again for such an invoice, and a payment of what is owed is recorded against it. Before, it was refunded as a duplicate payment. The invoice is never charged again automatically, and the payment the dispute took back is never applied again, even when its success event is replayed.
- Record a manual payment of the money you recovered another way, up to what is owed.
- Issue a credit note with an offset, up to what is owed. A credit to the customer's balance does not lower what is owed, because the customer keeps that credit for other invoices.
- Void the invoice.

When what is owed reaches 0, the block lifts with one `resolved` step, as for a payment. The dunning emails of such an invoice ask the customer to settle with you instead of linking to a pay page.

An invoice with a lost dispute cannot be refunded. Its refundable amount is 0, and a refund credit note is refused with `higher_than_remaining_invoice_amount`. A plan change is held back by it only while something is still owed. See [Outstanding invoices and lost disputes](/api/subscription-management#outstanding-invoices-and-lost-disputes).

Only the first loss counts. _Core 11.46.0._ `invoice.payment_dispute_lost` is sent, and the dunning policy started, only the first time a payment, or an invoice for the manual lose-dispute action, is lost. A repeat, such as a retried job or the provider sending the outcome again, changes nothing and sends nothing.

Before a dispute is lost, you hear about it through the [`invoice.payment_dispute_updated`](/api/webhook-events#invoices) webhook. Razorpay disputes send it, and from Core 11.46.0 Stripe disputes do too, when a dispute is opened or updated. Nothing is owed until the dispute is lost. An existing Stripe connection needs its webhook endpoint refreshed to send the new events. See [Stripe webhook setup](/integrations/stripe#webhook-setup).

A dispute lost before Core 11.45.0 has no recorded amount. Nozle counts what the invoice's provider payments recorded before the dispute still held (all its payments when it has no provider payment). An invoice with no payment before the dispute counts its whole total less the offsets issued before the dispute.

## The blocked customer

While blocked, the Engine refuses the customer with HTTP `402` and the code `customer_payment_overdue`. The customer is matched by `external_customer_id` or by the external id of its subscription. The state is read on every request, so paying lifts the block on the next call.

| Route | What the customer gets |
|---|---|
| `GET /can` | `402` `{"allowed": false, "reason": "customer_payment_overdue", "code": "customer_payment_overdue", "error": "..."}`. Checked once the customer's subscription is found. |
| `POST /usage/check`, `POST /usage/track` | `402` `{"code": "customer_payment_overdue", "error": "..."}`. Nothing is tracked. |
| `POST /events` | `402` `{"status": 402, "error": "...", "code": "customer_payment_overdue"}`. The event is not stored. |
| `POST /events/batch` | See [Batches](#batches). |
| LLM proxy (`/proxy/openai/*`, `/proxy/anthropic/*`) | `402` in the error shape of the provider, with `code` `customer_payment_overdue` in `error.code` and at the top level, for the `X-Nozle-Customer-Id` customer, before the provider is called. See [LLM proxy errors](/api/llm-proxy#errors). |

Events refused while the customer is blocked are not queued. Your app decides what the customer sees: the Node SDK returns `can()` as a denial with `reason: "customer_payment_overdue"`, so you can show a "pay your invoice" screen instead of an error. See [Node.js errors](/sdks/node/errors#customer-payment-overdue).

### Let a blocked customer pay

Your backend lets the customer pay from that screen:

1. List the customer's unpaid invoices: finalized invoices whose `payment_status` is `failed` or `pending`. Use `GET /invoices` with `external_customer_id`, `status=finalized` and `payment_status`, or the SDKs: Node `invoices.list(customerId, { status: 'finalized', paymentStatus: 'failed' })`, Python `invoices.list(customer_id, status="finalized", payment_status="failed")`, Go `ListInvoices(ctx, customerID, nozle.ListInvoicesParams{Status: "finalized", PaymentStatus: "failed"})`. Ask again with `pending`.
2. Create a [pay link](/guides/billing/checkout#pay-links) for each invoice and send the customer to it: `POST /invoices/{id}/payment_url`, or Node `checkoutInvoice(invoiceId, { returnUrl })`, Python `checkout_invoice(invoice_id)`, Go `CheckoutInvoice(ctx, invoiceID, key)`. Only the Node SDK takes a return URL. A pay link is refused with `422` `payment_already_processing` while an automatic debit of the invoice is waiting for the bank.
3. _Unreleased._ Or send the customer to the hosted customer portal: [`GET /customers/{external_customer_id}/portal_url`](/api-reference/customers/get-a-customer-portal-url) returns its `portal_url`, and **Pay** there opens the same payment page for each invoice the customer owes. See [Pay from the customer portal](/guides/billing/invoices#pay-from-the-customer-portal).

The block lifts on the next request once no invoice past its grace is owed. See [What resolves it](#what-resolves-it).

Paying does not lift `402` `customer_blocked`. That code means Nozle staff blocked the customer, and only they lift it. See [Customer blocked](/api/errors#customer-blocked).

### Batches

An events batch is not refused as a whole because one customer is blocked.

- Invalid events are refused one by one too (Engine 2.28.0), with their own codes in `error_details`. Before Engine 2.28.0 one invalid event failed the whole batch with `422` `validation_errors`.
- Then the events of blocked customers are set aside. The other events are stored and returned in `events` with `200`. The refused ones are listed by their position in the request under `error_details`:

```json
{
  "events": [ ... ],
  "error_details": {
    "2": { "external_customer_id": ["customer_payment_overdue"] }
  }
}
```

- When every event of the batch is refused for a blocked customer, the answer is `402` with `code` `customer_payment_overdue` and the same `error_details`, and nothing is stored. When nothing is stored and some refusals have another reason, the answer is `422` `validation_errors`.

### Where the policy stands

The customer shows where its timeline is, in the REST customer object (and so in the `customer.*` webhooks) and in the GraphQL `Customer` type:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `dunning_blocked_at` | When the customer was blocked. `null` when it is not. |
| `dunning_day` | The day of the timeline that the furthest unpaid invoice is on. `null` without a dunned invoice. |
| `dunning_next_action_at` | When the next step runs, a retry included (only after a soft decline). `null` when none is planned. |

They are updated each time the policy runs: every hour, when a dunned invoice changes and when a payment method is saved. The customer page of the dashboard shows the block (**Usage blocked for unpaid invoices**) or the dunning day and the next step (**In dunning**).

## The `dunning.step` webhook

Each step sends `dunning.step` (object key `dunning_step`). It carries the invoice and customer identifiers and:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `invoice_number` | Number of the unpaid invoice. |
| `external_customer_id` | Your id of the customer. |
| `dunning_campaign_code` | The campaign the invoice follows. |
| `step` | `payment_failed`, `reminder`, `final_reminder`, `end_action` or `resolved`. |
| `day` | The day of the timeline the step ran on. |
| `grace_days` | The grace of the plan behind the invoice. |
| `end_action` | `block`. |
| `started_at` | Day 0 of the invoice. |
| `deadline_at` | When the end action runs: `started_at` plus `grace_days`. |
| `next_action` | The next step (`reminder`, `final_reminder`, `end_action` or `retry`). `retry` only when the last decline is retried (a soft decline); after a hard decline or one that needs the customer, the next message step. `null` after the last one and on `resolved`. |
| `next_action_at` | When `next_action` runs. |
| `total_due_amount_cents`, `currency` | What is still owed on the invoice. After a [lost dispute](#lost-disputes), what is owed again (Core 11.45.0). |

`resolved` is sent once, when a dunned invoice no longer needs paying. `dunning_campaign.finished` is unchanged and still belongs to campaigns without an end action. See the [full payload](/api/webhook-events#dunningstep).

## Customer emails

The billing entity email `dunning.step` sends the customer an email at each step, in your brand. It is on for the default billing entity of a workspace created from Core 11.42.0 (see [the default policy](#the-default-policy)) and off elsewhere until you turn it on in the entity's `email_settings`. The wording escalates from the failed payment to the final reminder and the block. See [Customer emails](/guides/settings/customer-emails).

From Core 11.42.0 a customer gets one email per step, not one per invoice. It has a row for each invoice at that step (number, what it is for, amount; past three invoices the last row counts the rest) and the total still owed. With one invoice, the button opens its pay page, or your customer billing page when the invoice cannot be paid online. With several, it opens your customer billing page. When the billing page is needed and you have not set one, the email has no button. The `dunning.step` webhook is still sent per invoice. The "All good" email is sent once, when no owed invoice of the customer is left in a dunning timeline.

The customer gets one email on day 0, not two: when `dunning.step` is on, the failed payment email (`invoice.payment_failure`) is not sent for an invoice a dunning policy runs on. It is still sent for other invoices, and the `invoice.payment_failure` webhook is unchanged.
