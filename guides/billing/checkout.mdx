title: Checkout
description: Payment-aware Stripe-hosted and Razorpay checkout through an authenticated merchant backend
---

Nozle collects plan payments through the payment provider connected to your organization: Stripe or Razorpay. The flow is the same for both:

1. Customer selects a plan in your application
2. The React SDK calls your authenticated merchant callback with `{ planCode, returnUrl }`
3. Your backend derives the customer, validates the plan and return origin, and calls Nozle with `sk_`
4. Nozle keeps the current plan active and computes the exact invoice or proration
5. If money remains due, Nozle returns a provider result: a Stripe-hosted Checkout page URL (`type: "hosted"`), or a Razorpay order
6. Customer completes payment on the provider's page or modal
7. The provider confirms the payment to Nozle (a signed webhook, and for Razorpay also your verify call)
8. Nozle applies the paid plan change only after verified success

<Info>
Plan checkout is payment-gated: a paid plan change is not applied until the payment provider confirms payment. If prepaid credits reduce the exact amount due to zero, Nozle applies the change transactionally and returns `type: "completed"` without creating a provider session.
</Info>

A customer whose provider is not connected cannot pay: checkout returns `payment_provider_required`. Checkout charges only through the connection assigned to the customer and never picks another one; a customer with no assigned connection also gets `payment_provider_required`. Assign the connection in the dashboard, or send `billing_configuration.payment_provider` and `payment_provider_code` when you create the customer through the API; see the [Razorpay](/integrations/razorpay#assign-the-connection-to-customers) and [Stripe](/integrations/stripe#assign-the-connection-to-customers) guides. At checkout Nozle creates the customer in Stripe when it is missing, and returns `422 stripe_customer_required` if it cannot.

## Stripe

Every Stripe checkout is a Stripe-hosted Checkout Session: a new subscription's first invoice, an invoice, a paid plan change and the Entity subscription batch. Nozle returns `{"type": "hosted", "url", ...}` and you redirect the customer to `url`. There is no card form on your page, no Stripe publishable key and no Stripe.js to load. Nozle sends Adaptive Pricing disabled so the customer is charged in the invoice currency.

1. **Saved card first.** If the customer has a saved card that can be charged, Nozle charges it and answers `completed`, `processing` or `scheduled`. No page opens.
2. **Redirect.** Otherwise redirect the browser to `url`. The page is valid until `expires_at`.
3. **Return.** After paying, Stripe sends the customer to your `return_url`. If the customer leaves the page, Stripe sends them to `cancel_url`, or to the return URL when you sent none. Both may contain the literal `{CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}`, which Stripe replaces with the Checkout Session ID (the `checkout_id` of the result).
4. **Confirm.** The return only means the customer came back: Nozle applies the payment when Stripe's webhook arrives. For a new subscription or an invoice, wait for your `subscription.started` or invoice webhook, or reload the subscription or billing status: they have no status call. Only a paid upgrade has a status call: poll [`GET /checkout/{plan_upgrade_intent_id}`](/api/subscription-management#payment-status-and-recovery) with the customer and subscription scope. While the buyer is paying, `status` is `awaiting_payment` and the nested `checkout` is the hosted result again; `processing` means Stripe took the payment and Nozle is applying it; `succeeded` comes with a nested `completed`; `expired` means the Stripe session is no longer open. The `checkout_id` of a hosted result is the Stripe Checkout Session ID and is not a status ID.

```json
{
  "customer_id": "customer_123",
  "plan_code": "growth",
  "return_url": "https://app.example.com/billing/done?session={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}",
  "cancel_url": "https://app.example.com/billing"
}
```

`cancel_url` is accepted by `POST /checkout`. The Entity subscription endpoints take `return_url` only.

Credit top-ups and invoice payment URLs also open a Stripe-hosted page, valid for 24 hours.

From Core 11.15.4 (_Unreleased_):

- **Only the customer's payment methods.** The hosted page of an invoice offers only the payment methods set on the customer (`provider_payment_methods`), like the pay link and the automatic charge. It no longer offers every method of your Stripe account, so a customer cannot pay with one (Cash App, Klarna...) that a renewal could never charge again.
- **A card saved while paying is recorded.** When the customer pays on Stripe Checkout and the card is saved for later (an invoice pay link, a subscription or a plan change checkout), Nozle records it as the customer's payment method, like a card saved on the card page, with the India mandate when the charge carries one. Renewals then charge it automatically, and [`PaymentMethodDisplay`](/sdks/react/components/payment-method-display) shows it.
- **Return URL of a pay link.** GraphQL `generatePaymentUrl(returnUrl:)` and REST [`POST /invoices/{id}/payment_url`](#pay-links) take an optional return URL: where the provider sends the customer after paying (Stripe success and cancel URL, Razorpay payment link callback). The Node SDK's `checkoutInvoice(invoiceId, { returnUrl })` sends it from 0.23.0. An invoice checkout given a return URL no longer reuses an open Checkout Session that returns elsewhere.

A card issued in India needs an e-mandate before Nozle can charge it automatically, and Stripe Checkout cannot register one. A first payment through Stripe Checkout therefore leaves an Indian card without a mandate: the customer pays that first invoice by hand and later invoices use the hosted page until a card is saved on Nozle's card page. With a publishable key, the invoice pay link of a customer from India opens the card page too, so paying through it registers the mandate. When the Stripe connection has a publishable key, card setup links (not checkout) open that Nozle-hosted page and return the customer to the return URL with `nozle_card=succeeded`, `processing` or `failed`. See [Indian cards and automatic payments](/integrations/stripe#indian-cards-and-automatic-payments).

### Card page in your app

The card page can run in a frame in your own app, so the customer does not leave it. It is the page of card setup links, [Manage payment method](/api/manage-payment-method) and invoice pay links, not the result of `POST /checkout`, which stays a Stripe Checkout Session. Add `embed=1` to the page `url`, or use [`NozleCardSetup`](/sdks/react/components/nozle-card-setup) (available from `@nozle-js/react` 0.20.0). Nozle allows framing only from the origin of the `return_url` you passed when you created the link, and the page posts `{ source: "nozle", type: "card_setup", status, mode }` to your page, where `status` is `ready`, `succeeded`, `processing`, `failed` or `error`. See [Show the card page in your app](/integrations/stripe#show-the-card-page-in-your-app). If the connection's publishable key is rejected by Stripe, the page sends the customer to Stripe Checkout instead.

### Required Stripe webhook setup

When you connect a Stripe account, Nozle registers a Stripe webhook endpoint for that account and stores Stripe's webhook signing secret. Your Stripe account must allow webhook endpoint creation.

The webhook target is:

```text
https://api.nozle.app/core/webhooks/stripe/{organization_id}?code={stripe_provider_code}
```

Stripe events Nozle handles:

- `payment_intent.succeeded`
- `payment_intent.payment_failed`
- `payment_intent.canceled`
- `setup_intent.succeeded`
- `payment_method.detached` (received, but it does not clear the stored payment method today)
- `customer.updated`
- `refund.updated`
- `refund.failed`
- `charge.dispute.closed`

Nozle subscribes to these nine events only (the older `charge.refund.updated` is still accepted for one release). Without this webhook, Stripe may collect payment but Nozle is not told. Checkout status reads the Checkout Session from Stripe and reports `processing` once it is complete, but it never settles a payment, so only the webhook does. Self-hosters upgrading from an earlier release run `payment_providers:refresh_stripe_webhook_events` once so existing endpoints subscribe to the new refund events. See the [Stripe integration](/integrations/stripe) for setup, flows and limits.

Retrying a new-subscription checkout for the same plan while its subscription is still incomplete reopens the first checkout. A different plan replaces the abandoned checkout and returns the new one; `409 subscription_checkout_in_progress` remains only while the provider is still processing a payment for the old checkout. An unpaid plan or single Entity checkout expires after 23 hours: the subscription is canceled with `cancelation_reason: timeout`, the invoice is closed and the credits it used are returned. See [Unpaid checkout expiry](/api/create-checkout#unpaid-checkout-expiry).

## Razorpay

Connect Razorpay in the dashboard with its key ID, key secret and webhook secret. Optional settings turn on auto-pay mandates (with a maximum mandate amount). Razorpay supports only INR. Nozle does not register the Razorpay webhook for you: copy the webhook URL Nozle shows for the connection into your Razorpay dashboard, with the same webhook secret. See the [Razorpay integration](/integrations/razorpay) for setup, events and limits.

```text
https://api.nozle.app/core/webhooks/razorpay/{payment_provider_id}
```

Nozle checks the `X-Razorpay-Signature` header against the webhook secret and de-duplicates on `X-Razorpay-Event-Id`. On `payment.authorized`, `payment.captured`, `payment.failed`, `order.paid` and `payment_link.paid`, Nozle fetches the payment from Razorpay and settles the checkout once the payment is captured. Refund, mandate and dispute events are also handled.

A Razorpay checkout runs in four steps:

1. **Create.** Checkout returns `{"type": "razorpay", "checkout_id", "key_id", "order_id", "amount_cents", "currency", "expires_at", "invoice_id"}`. A paid upgrade has `plan_upgrade_intent_id` instead of `invoice_id`. A mandate checkout (`register_mandate`) opens no modal: it answers `type: hosted` with `payment_url`, Nozle's auto-pay setup page, and `checkout_id` to poll (see [Recurring payments with auto-pay](/integrations/razorpay#recurring-payments-with-auto-pay)). If the order is not ready yet, or has expired, the result is `{"type": "processing", "checkout_id", "status"}` instead. A bulk Entity checkout first answers `{"entity_subscription_checkout": {"type": "processing", "status": "open", "checkout_id": "..."}}`. A `processing` result carries an internal operation status such as `ready`, not the state of the checkout.
2. **Pay.** Load `https://checkout.razorpay.com/v1/checkout.js` and open `new window.Razorpay({ key: key_id, order_id, handler })`.
3. **Verify.** Send the three fields from the handler to your backend, which calls `POST /checkout/{checkout_id}/verify` with `razorpay_order_id`, `razorpay_payment_id` and `razorpay_signature`. A missing field returns `400`. Nozle checks that the order matches the checkout and that the signature is the HMAC-SHA256 of `order_id|payment_id` with your key secret; a mismatch returns `422 validation_errors` with `invalid_checkout_signature`. Nozle then fetches the payment from Razorpay and settles only a `captured` payment. The response is the checkout status.
4. **Poll.** Call `GET /checkout/{checkout_id}` until `status` and `fulfillment_status` are both `succeeded`. Each call reconciles with Razorpay.

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `status` | `succeeded`, `needs_review`, `failed`, `expired`, `awaiting_payment`, `processing` |
| `fulfillment_status` | `pending`, `processing`, `succeeded` |

The nested `checkout` of the status response is present while the checkout is `awaiting_payment` and also for an `expired` unpaid order. For the expired order it is `{"type": "processing", "status": "ready"}`, so do not reopen the modal for it. A status call without a customer and subscription scope needs a secret key; an unknown ID returns `404 checkout_session_not_found`. The React [`CheckoutButton`](/sdks/react/components) runs steps 2 to 4 for you when `BillingProvider` has `verifyCheckout` and `getCheckoutStatus` callbacks; it polls 30 times, one second apart.

To retry a new-subscription checkout, call checkout again for the same plan: while the first order is unpaid it returns that same order. The React "Payment failed. You can retry checkout." button does this. See [Retry a new-subscription checkout](/integrations/razorpay#retry-a-new-subscription-checkout).

`POST /checkout` never returns `type: "hosted"` for Razorpay. A Razorpay payment link comes from an invoice payment URL (`POST /invoices/{id}/payment_url`) or a credit top-up purchase, as an `rzp.io` `payment_url` with no checkout ID to poll. Redirect the customer to it; the `payment_link.paid` webhook settles it.

## Pay links

A pay link pays one invoice. Create it with `POST /invoices/{id}/payment_url` (REST), `generatePaymentUrl` (GraphQL) or, through the Engine, `POST /checkout` with `invoice_id` (the Node SDK's `checkoutInvoice`).

```json
{
  "return_url": "https://app.example.com/billing/paid"
}
```

`return_url` is optional and validated like every checkout return URL (see [Return URLs](#return-urls-and-your-organizations-origins)); a refused one answers `422` `return_url: [invalid_or_untrusted]`. Without it, the customer stays on the provider's page after paying. An unexpired pay link keeps the return URL it was created with.

A pay link is refused with `422` and `error_details.base`:

- `payment_already_processing` while an automatic debit of the invoice is waiting for the bank (for example an Indian card during the bank's notice), so the customer is not charged twice. Wait for the debit's outcome.
- `invoice_carried_forward` when the amount due is below the provider's minimum charge and was carried to the next invoice. See [Amounts below the provider minimum](/guides/billing/invoices#amounts-below-the-provider-minimum).
- `invalid_invoice_status_or_payment_status` when the invoice is paid, voided, draft, closed, failed or has nothing due.

## API shape

Plan checkout:

```json
{
  "customer_id": "customer_123",
  "plan_code": "growth",
  "return_url": "https://app.example.com/settings/billing"
}
```

For a customer with no subscription, a free plan, a plan with a trial or a plan billed in arrears returns `422 checkout_not_required`: start it with [`subscribe`](/api/subscribe) instead. For an existing subscriber, an immediate plan change with nothing to pay returns a completed response, including a paid upgrade fully covered by prepaid credits. See [Create checkout](/api/create-checkout). Downgrades and other cycle-end transitions return a scheduled response from the same `/checkout` endpoint; browser sessions never call `/billing/upgrade` or `/subscriptions/change`.

## Return URLs and your organization's origins

A checkout `return_url` (or `success_url`) must be an absolute HTTPS URL on an allowed origin. An origin is the scheme and host, plus the port when it is not 443, with no path: `https://app.acme.com` or `https://app.acme.com:8443`. Core accepts the origins of the Nozle deployment and the origins you register for your own organization, for `POST /checkout_sessions`, Entity checkouts and Entity subscription checkout batches. Another organization's origin is refused with `422` and `return_url: [invalid_or_untrusted]`.

Register your origins with `PUT /organizations` (REST) or `updateOrganization` (GraphQL), which needs the `developers:manage` permission:

```json
{
  "organization": {
    "checkout_return_origins": ["https://app.acme.com"],
    "checkout_default_return_url": "https://app.acme.com/billing/done"
  }
}
```

- `checkout_return_origins`: up to 20 HTTPS origins, each a scheme and host only (no path, query or user info). They are stored in lowercase without a trailing slash. An invalid value answers `422` with `checkout_return_origins: [value_is_invalid]`.
- `checkout_default_return_url`: an HTTPS URL on one of the allowed origins, or `null`. A URL off the allowed origins answers `422` with `checkout_default_return_url: [invalid_or_untrusted]`.
- GraphQL: `checkoutReturnOrigins` and `checkoutDefaultReturnUrl` on `updateOrganization` and `CurrentOrganization`.
- `GET /organizations` returns both settings.

When a checkout gives no return URL, the customer returns to your default return URL, else to the first registered origin, else to the payment provider's success URL.

The Engine's `POST /checkout` and `POST /manage-payment-method` routes send `return_url` to Core as it is and apply no list of their own, so these per-organization settings decide. A refused URL answers `400` with `code: invalid_return_url`; see [create checkout](/api/create-checkout).

You can also set both in the dashboard under **Developers > Checkout return**. Only members who can manage developer settings and update the organization can change them. The page validates with Core's messages: `value_is_invalid` for an origin that is not `https://host` or `https://host:port`, `value_is_too_long` for more than 20 origins, and `invalid_or_untrusted` for a default URL off the allowed origins.

## Response

```json
{
  "type": "hosted",
  "url": "https://checkout.stripe.com/c/pay/cs_...",
  "payment_url": "https://checkout.stripe.com/c/pay/cs_...",
  "checkout_id": "cs_...",
  "invoice_id": "invoice_uuid",
  "amount_cents": 19900,
  "currency": "USD",
  "expires_at": "2026-11-01T12:00:00Z"
}
```

The browser calls your authenticated merchant backend, which derives the Nozle customer and starts checkout with a restricted `sk_`. A Nozle publishable key is valid only for the plan catalog. Your app needs no Stripe key: it redirects to `url`.

Credit-funded completion response:

```json
{
  "type": "completed",
  "status": "succeeded",
  "payment_source": "credits",
  "plan_code": "growth",
  "amount_cents": 0,
  "currency": "USD"
}
```

Scheduled transition response:

```json
{
  "type": "scheduled",
  "status": "pending",
  "subscription_id": "subscription_uuid",
  "plan_code": "starter",
  "transition_direction": "downgrade",
  "transition_policy": "next_period",
  "effective_at": "2026-11-01T00:00:00Z"
}
```

`effective_at` uses the customer's timezone offset, for example `2026-11-01T00:00:00.000000+05:30`.

Changing an existing subscription uses the [default plan change policy](/api/plan-transition-policies#the-default-policy) (an upgrade now, prorated; a downgrade at the end of the period) or a policy you set for that pair of plans. An immediate downgrade, a hidden or older plan, or a trial needs a pair policy: without one, checkout returns `422 plan_transition_policy_required`. See [Create checkout](/api/create-checkout) for every result type and error.

## React implementation

### Option A -- CheckoutButton (simplest)

```tsx
import { CheckoutButton } from '@nozle-js/react'

<CheckoutButton
  planCode="growth"
  returnUrl="https://app.example.com/settings/billing"
  label="Subscribe to Growth"
/>
```

For a `hosted` result the button redirects the browser to `url`. Nothing else to mount.

### Option B -- Custom flow

Call your backend, then redirect to the `url` it returns:

```tsx
const handleCheckout = async () => {
  const checkout = await startCheckout('growth')
  if (checkout.type === 'hosted') window.location.assign(checkout.url)
}
```

Show success from your return page, or by reading billing status for the customer. Do not trust the return alone: Nozle applies the change when Stripe's webhook arrives.

## Server-side plan changes

For B2B scenarios where payment is already handled outside checkout:

```typescript
import { Nozle } from '@nozle-js/node'

const nozle = new Nozle() // reads NOZLE_SECRET_KEY

await nozle.customers.upsert({ externalId: 'customer_123', name: 'Acme' });
const result = await nozle.subscribe('customer_123', 'growth');
// { subscription_id: 'sub_...', status: 'active' }
```

`subscribe()` requires a secret key (`sk_`) and an existing customer: an unknown customer returns `404 customer_not_found`. Checkout should be used when Nozle needs to collect payment before activating a paid plan.

## Mixed Entity plan checkout

Seat-based products can sell different Entity plans in one invoice and one payment. Every plan in the basket must require an up-front payment: a free, trial or pay-in-arrears plan is refused with `paid_plan_required`. Your backend first creates or reuses active placeholder Entities, then calls the bulk Entity checkout endpoint with the selected plan for each placeholder.

```text
Customer workspace
├── seat_pro_001 -> Pro monthly
├── seat_pro_002 -> Pro monthly
└── seat_max_001 -> Max monthly
           │
           └── one Nozle invoice -> one Stripe or Razorpay checkout
```

Keep the secret key in your backend. The browser should send only merchant-facing intent, such as `{ proSeats: 2, maxSeats: 1 }`; your backend derives the customer, creates stable Entity IDs, validates the return origin, and calls Nozle.

Use `billing_time: "anniversary"` when the first successful purchase should establish the customer's renewal day. Later Entity purchases align to that day and use Nozle's invoice proration.

See [Entity Subscriptions](/api/entity-subscriptions#purchase-multiple-entity-plans-together) for the complete request and response.
