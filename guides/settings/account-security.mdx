These settings are personal. They apply to your own sign-in, not to the workspace.

## Profile

Your email is used to sign in and to attribute every change you make.

One Nozle account exists per email, whatever the case of its letters. Signing up again with the same email, even as `Name@example.com` instead of `name@example.com`, is refused with `user_already_exists` and creates no second account. This applies to email sign-up, Google and Okta (Core 11.46.0).

### Google sign-in

_Unreleased._ Nozle matches a Google sign-in to an account by email, so it refuses a Google account whose email Google has not verified (`google_email_not_verified`). Emails match in any case on password sign-in, password reset and Google. Any invalid Google token (wrong audience, issuer, expiry or signature) answers `invalid_google_token`. Google sign-in stays switched off in the dashboard until the release is configured, and cannot be turned on as a workspace's only sign-in method while Google credentials are missing (`google_auth_missing_setup`).

## Password

Nozle changes passwords through a verified email link, never in the browser. Choose **Send reset link** and follow the link in the email. Your current password keeps working until you use the link.

- **Only the newest link works.** A reset link is valid for 30 minutes. Asking for a new link cancels every earlier one, so use the link in the latest email.
- **The same answer for every email.** A reset request, from this page or from the **Forgot your password?** page, answers the same way whether or not the email has a Nozle account. Only an email with an account receives a link.
- **A new password signs out your other sessions.** Every browser and device signed in with the old password is signed out. The browser where you set the new password stays signed in.

## Two-factor authentication

_Unreleased._ With two-factor authentication on, signing in with your password also asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app such as Google Authenticator, Authy or 1Password. A stolen password alone then cannot open your account. It covers every workspace you belong to.

### Turn it on

1. Open **Your account** and choose **Set up** under **Two-factor authentication**.
2. In your authenticator app, add an account and enter the setup key shown on the page. On a phone, **open the authenticator app directly** adds it for you.
3. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows and choose **Turn on**.
4. Save the ten backup codes: copy them or choose **Download**. They are shown only once.

Nothing changes for your sign-in until step 3 succeeds.

### Sign in

After your email and password, enter the current code from your authenticator app, or one of your backup codes. Each backup code works once. The code step lasts 5 minutes; after that, sign in with your password again. Setting a new password from a reset link also asks for a code before it signs you in.

A code is accepted for about 30 seconds either side of the time on your phone, and only once. If a code is refused, wait for the next one.

### Backup codes

Use a backup code when you cannot use your authenticator app. **Create new codes** replaces all of them; the old ones stop working. Both actions ask for a current code or a backup code.

If you lose your authenticator app and your backup codes, ask an admin of your workspace to reset your two-factor authentication (see [Team access](/guides/settings/team-access#two-factor-authentication)).

### Turn it off

Choose **Turn off** and enter a code or a backup code. A workspace that requires two-factor authentication will ask you to set it up again.

### Google and Okta

Signing in with Google or Okta never asks for a Nozle code. Your identity provider protects those sign-ins, so turn on two-step verification there. They also count as protected in a workspace that requires two-factor authentication.

### Sign-in limits for codes

The code step accepts 5 codes per account and 100 requests per IP address every 15 minutes. Past the limit, wait before trying again.

## Sign in with an email link

_Unreleased._ You can sign in without your password. On the sign-in page choose **Email link**, enter your work email and choose **Email me a sign-in link**. Open the link from the email to sign in.

- **The link works once and expires after 15 minutes.** Asking for a new link cancels the earlier one, so use the link in the latest email. An expired or used link says so and sends you back to the sign-in page to ask for a new one.
- **The same answer for every email.** The page says **Check your inbox** whether or not the email has a Nozle account. Only an account that can sign in with its email receives a link: one with an active membership in a workspace where email sign-in is allowed. A workspace that allows only Okta sends no link.
- **It is an email sign-in.** The link signs you in as your password would, so the same rules apply: two-factor authentication still asks for your code, and a workspace that requires two-factor authentication still asks you to set it up. Anyone who can read your email can sign in with the link, as they could reset your password.
- **Open it in the browser you used.** After sign-in Nozle continues to the page you were on, such as a coding agent's approval page. A link opened in another browser lands on the default start page.
- Each request is recorded as `user.magic_link_requested` in the [security log](/guides/settings/audit-logs) of every workspace you belong to.

## Active sessions

_Unreleased._ **Your account** lists every browser and device signed in to your account, most recently active first. Each row shows the browser, the operating system or device, the sign-in method, when it was last active and the IP address it signed in from. Your current browser is marked **This browser**. Nozle does not show a location.

- **Sign out** ends one session. Signing out the one you are using signs you out here.
- **Sign out others** ends every session except this one. It cannot be undone.

A session ends when any of these happens:

- **30 minutes without activity.** An open tab that you use keeps its session going. An idle tab is signed out on its own.
- **12 hours after you signed in**, however often you use it. Sign in again to continue.
- **You sign it out**, or an admin of your workspace signs you out (see [Sign a member out](/guides/settings/team-access#sign-a-member-out)).
- **You change your password.** A password reset ends every session of your account. The browser where you set the new password signs in again.

A request with an ended session answers `expired_jwt_token` (HTTP 401). Sessions belong to your account, so ending one affects it in every workspace you belong to. After the release every member signs in once more.

## Delete your account

_Unreleased._ **Your account** has a **Delete account** button under the danger zone. It deletes your own sign-in. Any member can do it when nothing below blocks it. If something does, the page lists each reason and names the workspace.

What happens depends on each workspace you belong to:

- **Other members remain.** You leave the workspace and it stays with them. You cannot leave it if you are its last admin: make another member an admin first.
- **You are the only member.** The workspace closes, and it must have no billing history. These block the deletion: subscriptions, invoices, payments, credit notes, credit transactions, payment requests or usage events (the sandbox never counts), a paid Nozle plan, invoice or payment (the free plan does not count), and a connected payment provider (disconnect it first).

To delete, type your email in the dialog (the letters can be in any case) and confirm. Nozle checks the rules again at that moment. If something changed, the deletion is refused with `account_not_deletable` or `email_mismatch` and the page shows the current reasons.

What it removes:

- You are signed out on every device and every membership is revoked.
- Your password, sign-in links, two-factor setup, devices and stored email are removed. The account row stays under a placeholder address, so audit history is intact.
- A workspace that closes expires its API keys, deletes its webhook endpoints, cancels its pending invitations, retires its sandbox, ends its free Nozle plan and erases its Nozle customer.
- Invitations sent to your email are marked as deleted.

You can sign up again with the same email. It creates a new account with none of the old data. The deletion is recorded as `user.account_deleted` in the security log. To delete a workspace without deleting your account, see [Delete a workspace](/guides/settings/delete-workspace).

## Sign-in limits

To slow down password guessing and mass sign-ups, the sign-in, sign-in link, sign-up, invitation and password-reset forms accept a limited number of requests. The rows marked Core 11.54.0 are new in that release. The sign-in link rows are _Unreleased_.

| Request | Per email | Per IP address |
|---|---|---|
| Sign in | 10 per 15 minutes | 100 per 15 minutes |
| Sign in with Google (Core 11.54.0) | | 100 per 15 minutes |
| Start Okta sign-in (Core 11.54.0) | 10 per 15 minutes | 100 per 15 minutes |
| Finish Okta sign-in (Core 11.54.0) | | 100 per 15 minutes |
| Accept an invitation (Core 11.54.0) | 10 per 15 minutes | 100 per 15 minutes |
| Accept an invitation with Google (Core 11.54.0) | | 100 per 15 minutes |
| Accept an invitation with Okta (Core 11.54.0) | | 100 per 15 minutes |
| Sign up (Core 11.54.0) | 10 per hour | 30 per hour |
| Sign up with Google (Core 11.54.0) | | 30 per hour |
| Password-reset request | 5 per hour | 30 per hour |
| Set a new password from a reset link (Core 11.54.0) | | 30 per hour |
| Sign-in link request (_Unreleased_) | 5 per hour | 30 per hour |
| Sign in with an emailed link (_Unreleased_) | | 30 per hour |

Past a limit the form shows **Please wait before trying again**. Wait a few minutes, then try again. The limits apply whether or not the email has an account. A request that tries to do two of these at once is refused. The [API rate limits](/api/rate-limits) are separate.

## Sign-in history

The history lists your recent sign-ins and marks the unsuccessful ones. If several failed attempts happened just before your last successful sign-in, the page warns you. Reset your password if that was not you.

If your role cannot view the security log, the history is hidden. The security log is available in every workspace. Admins can see everyone's sign-ins under [Audit logs](/guides/settings/audit-logs).

## Sign out

Signing out ends this session and returns you to the sign-in screen. Every other open tab signs out too. Your data and workspace access stay as they were.
